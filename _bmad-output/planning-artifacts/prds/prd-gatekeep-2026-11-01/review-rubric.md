# PRD Quality Review — Gatekeep

## Overall verdict
The thesis, the honesty about why Gatekeep exists, the deferral discipline in §6–§7 and the counter-metrics all hold up, and this is a substantive PRD with little furniture. The risk is in the identity model and the security-critical flows: Deployment-level authority and Admin sign-in are undefined, the key-compromise journey contradicts its FRs, and the passkey, MFA and recovery rules don't add up. Fix those three and settle Open Questions 3 and 4 before this goes to architecture, because downstream work would otherwise invent the answers. Scope is also well short of the "smallest product that is safe to depend on" that the PRD promises.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The big decisions are stated as decisions. §1 says plainly that "No specific failure of Keycloak, Clerk or any other product drives it" and that production, not learning, sets the quality bar. §6 and §7.1 make firm calls, and SM-C3 ("A missed date is better than an unsafe launch") names what is being traded away. Few PRDs are this direct about motive.

The gaps are where the hardest trade-offs are recorded but not decided. Relying on self-assessment alone is the largest security decision in the document, and it appears only as a risk row. Open Questions 3 and 4 shape the architecture, yet they sit unranked with no owner, no deadline and no statement of what they block. There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at all, so the real tensions carry no flag where a reader meets them.

### Findings
- **[high]** Hosting viability is left open under a firm target (§11 Q4, NFR-10, A-6) — "Is the on-premises server reachable from the public internet, and does it have redundant power and network?" decides whether a production identity platform can run there at all, not only whether NFR-10 is realistic. FR-1 and the §9 outage risks all assume the answer. *Fix:* resolve Q4 before architecture, or mark it as a blocking gate with the fallback hosting decision named.
- **[high]** Availability, restore and upgrade targets don't fit together (NFR-10, NFR-13, FR-3) — 99.9% allows "about 43 minutes of downtime" a month, while one permitted restore takes "under 1 hour". A single failed upgrade (UJ-4) therefore breaks the month's target, and the PRD doesn't say whether planned upgrades count as downtime. *Fix:* state whether planned maintenance is excluded, and either lower the target or shorten the restore time for a single-server Deployment.
- **[high]** Forgoing independent security review is not stated as a decision (§9 "One Operator, no second reviewer", NFR-8, SM-3) — the risk is "The self-assessment can miss what its author missed", and the mitigation is the same self-assessment plus checklists. An external penetration test or second reviewer is never considered or rejected. *Fix:* decide explicitly, either requiring an independent review before real Users or recording that it was given up and why, and accept the residual risk by name.
- **[medium]** Open Question 2 is half-answered and untracked (§11 Q2) — "Assumed: apps use another login until SM-3 passes" is an inference with no `[ASSUMPTION]` tag, and SM-1 requires "no fallback login in use" without saying when that switch happens. *Fix:* tag and index the assumption and define the cutover sequence, including what happens to apps' existing accounts.
- **[medium]** Open Questions have no decision path (§11) — none has an owner, a due stage or a stated consequence. Q3 changes the Admin model, and Q1 changes a v1 dependency. *Fix:* add "blocks" and "decide by" to each, and add `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at NFR-10, NFR-8 and FR-39.

## Substance over theater — strong
There are two protagonists, Esm and Maya, and both drive requirements. No Differentiation section pads the document, and §1 explicitly refuses a gap claim. The Vision couldn't be swapped into another PRD. NFRs mostly carry product-specific content, such as NFR-2's "parameters can be raised without resetting Users" and NFR-5's "Hiding something in the Admin Dashboard isn't a control." The counter-metrics are real constraints rather than decoration.

### Findings
- **[low]** NFR-9 is close to boilerplate (NFR-9) — "A security fix process exists" sets no response time and doesn't say what the process produces. *Fix:* require a written runbook and a time bound for critical fixes.
- **[low]** SM-7 can only be judged by its subject (SM-7) — "Esm can explain and operate each v1 protocol flow" is honest about the Learn goal but is self-graded, and §9 also relies on it to mitigate unreviewed generated code. *Fix:* make it evidence-based, for example a written walkthrough per flow, produced without reference to the generated code.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear: build it to own and learn it, but hold it to a production bar, and ship "the smallest product that is safe to depend on". The Primary SMs validate that thesis well, since SM-1, SM-2 and SM-3 measure real dependence, isolation and security rather than activity. Counter-metrics are named and pointed at the right temptations. The MVP kind is effectively problem-solving, and §7 is disciplined against the full design.

Scope doesn't follow from the thesis, though. §7.1 says "Everything in §4 and §5": 44 FRs and 19 NFRs for a sole builder with "1–3 web apps at launch" (A-12), with no priority order inside v1. SM-C1 warns that "A larger Gatekeep is more to secure", yet several FRs serve no journey and no stated need. §1's "comparable in scope to Auth0" also sits awkwardly beside "smallest".

### Findings
- **[high]** v1 exceeds the stated minimum, with no sequencing (§1, §7.1, A-12) — candidates that trace to no UJ include three-role multi-Admin invites (FR-39) when Esm is the sole Operator, native Applications (FR-11) when A-12 says web apps, five quickstarts across five stacks (FR-43), theming with preview (FR-34), configuration export (FR-41), per-Application token settings and three social providers (FR-20). Each adds attack surface against the PRD's own SM-C1. *Fix:* test each FR against "needed to be safe to depend on", move the rest to v2 and order what remains into milestones, with the first production cutover as the target.
- **[medium]** "Proves itself" has no gate (§1 "until v1 has proved itself", §7.1 "after production proves stable") — SM-1 measures 90 days on Gatekeep but sets no availability, incident or data-loss condition, so a shaky 90 days still passes. *Fix:* attach NFR-10 attainment and zero severe incidents to SM-1, and name that as the v2 entry condition.
- **[medium]** SM-5 omits the failed-upgrade journey (SM-5, UJ-4, FR-3) — rehearsals cover "A key rotation and a full backup restore" but not an upgrade failing partway, which is the actual UJ-4 scenario. *Fix:* add a failed-upgrade rehearsal and link SM-5 to FR-3.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
The FR format is good, and many consequences are sharp: FR-15's token family revocation, FR-21's subdomain binding, and FR-44's "don't appear as disabled or placeholder pages". An engineer could write tests for those today. In a security product, though, too many values and interactions are left open, and the most important flows are the least defined. The misses below are the ones that would become wrong stories.

### Findings
- **[critical]** UJ-3 contradicts its FRs and cannot be completed as written (UJ-3, FR-31, FR-37, FR-38) — UJ-3 says Esm "rotates the key" and "tokens signed by the old key are rejected", but FR-37 says "Tokens signed by the previous key verify until they expire". Only FR-38's revoke rejects them, and UJ-3 never mentions revoking. "Apps keep working" is also undefined once valid access tokens fail. "Revokes affected sessions" has no FR, because FR-31 acts on one User at a time. The "alert" that starts the journey has no FR either, since FR-6 provides only a health signal. *Fix:* rewrite UJ-3 as rotate then revoke, add Tenant-wide Session and refresh token revocation, define the effect on in-flight tokens and JWKS caches, and either add alerting or start the journey from Logs alone.
- **[critical]** Passkey, MFA and recovery rules don't compose (Glossary Connection and Factor, UJ-2, FR-21, FR-25, FR-26, FR-27) — "passkey" is a Connection, while WebAuthn is a Factor. FR-27 issues recovery codes "when first enrolling a Factor", but Maya in UJ-2 creates a passkey, which is a first factor under FR-21, so as written she may hold no recovery codes and the edge case fails. With MFA set to Always, the PRD doesn't say whether a passkey satisfies MFA, which UJ-2's "one passkey prompt" requires. Passkey-only and code-only sign-up is also unspecified, since FR-17 covers only password sign-up. *Fix:* define the relationship between passkey and WebAuthn Factor, state whether a passkey satisfies MFA, issue recovery codes on first passkey creation, and specify a recovery path for every account type.
- **[high]** FR-25 has no testable consequences (FR-25) — it is the only one of 44 FRs without a Consequences block, and it governs MFA. Nothing covers when enforcement happens, how existing Users are treated on a change from Never to Always, or whether disallowing a Factor strands Users who rely on it. *Fix:* add consequences for all three.
- **[high]** Restore semantics are unsafe and conflict with the journey (UJ-4, FR-4, NFR-13) — UJ-4 promises "no credentials lost", but NFR-13 allows 24 hours of data loss, so passwords, passkeys and resets from that window are gone. Restoring can also bring back keys, refresh tokens, Sessions and blocks that were revoked after the backup, which is dangerous after a compromise. FR-4 says backups don't expose private keys but not where the decryption key lives, and a lost key means an unrecoverable backup. *Fix:* limit the UJ-4 claim to the backup point, add a post-restore step to re-apply or force revocations, and specify how the backup encryption key is held off-server.
- **[high]** The security gate isn't measurable (NFR-8, SM-3) — "a structured self-assessment of v1 against the OWASP ASVS" names no ASVS level, and the standard's requirements aren't graded "critical and high", so pass and fail depend on undefined severity. SM-3 also claims to validate "NFR-1 to NFR-9" while testing only NFR-8. *Fix:* name the ASVS level and version, define the severity scale, and have SM-3 list evidence for the other NFRs.
- **[high]** Log immutability conflicts with erasure, retention and Tenant deletion (Glossary Log, FR-7, FR-40, NFR-14, NFR-15) — Logs are "immutable", yet they age out at 30 days, NFR-14 erases a User's personal data, and FR-7 deletes a Tenant's data "under a stated retention rule" that is stated nowhere. An Owner can therefore remove a Tenant's audit trail by deleting it. *Fix:* state the retention rule, define how erasure treats Log identifiers, and define what survives Tenant deletion.
- **[high]** Protocol conformance can pass vacuously (FR-23, SM-4) — "the profiles it claims" lists none, so claiming none passes. "OAuth 2.x" also doesn't say whether userinfo, revocation or introspection endpoints exist, or whether access tokens are JWTs, although UJ-3 assumes signed tokens. *Fix:* list the conformance profiles and required endpoints in FR-23.
- **[medium]** FR-8 tests something Gatekeep doesn't control (FR-8, UJ-5) — "rejected by every other Tenant's APIs" describes Esm's own resource servers, which do the validating, and no FR provides validation guidance or libraries. The test suite also covers "every Management API endpoint" while the requirement spans Hosted Login and the Admin Dashboard, even though this is the product's top risk. *Fix:* reword to audience and issuer guarantees that Gatekeep can test, require validation in quickstarts, and extend the suite to all three surfaces.
- **[medium]** FR-3 has an open escape clause (FR-3) — "unless the release notes say otherwise" lets any release invalidate Sessions and tokens, "supported versions" is undefined, and UJ-4's backup-first step isn't enforced. *Fix:* define the supported upgrade path, restrict breaking changes to named conditions, and decide whether upgrade requires a recent backup.
- **[medium]** Security parameters have no bounds (FR-12, FR-19, FR-22, FR-28, FR-35, NFR-6) — "expire", "limited in attempts", "configurable overlap", "recent authentication" and "Sensitive endpoints" carry no default or limit. FR-28 also doesn't address an attacker deliberately locking out Maya, or how lockout screens avoid breaking FR-17's non-enumeration rule. *Fix:* add a table of defaults and permitted ranges, and name the sensitive endpoints.
- **[medium]** Social and password collisions are undefined (FR-20, FR-17, §7.1) — a social identity is "never linked automatically" and account linking is v2, but the PRD doesn't say what happens when a Google email matches an existing User: a second User, a refusal, or something else. *Fix:* specify the v1 behaviour, including how it preserves non-enumeration.
- **[medium]** Prod wildcard rule weakens exact matching (FR-11, FR-7) — "matched exactly, without wildcards in Prod Tenants" implies wildcards elsewhere, and Prod is an editable label, so relabelling removes the protection. This undercuts UJ-1's open-redirect edge case. *Fix:* ban wildcards everywhere, or define non-Prod limits and restrict and log label changes.
- **[medium]** NFR-11's load is not a load (NFR-11, NFR-12) — "expected load" points to "under 10,000 Users", a stored count that gives no requests per second, so p95 has no test condition. *Fix:* state peak sign-ins per second and token requests.
- **[medium]** Logout leaves token behaviour open (FR-24, FR-32) — FR-24's only consequence is the redirect, not whether refresh tokens end or Applications are told. Similarly, FR-32's "when RBAC is enabled for the API" has no FR that enables it. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-24 and an RBAC setting to FR-16 or FR-32.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§6 does real work, particularly "no Rules and Hooks screens, ever" and the impersonation rationale. §7.1's section table is the document's best feature: it accounts for every designed area, and §7.2 does the same for screens. Per-FR "Out of Scope" notes are placed where omissions might be assumed.

Open-items density is 4 Open Questions, 11 assumptions and 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, or 15 items. Most are acceptable for a personal project, but this PRD gates real credentials. Q3, Q4 and A-6 block the next stage and shouldn't count as ordinary open items.

### Findings
- **[high]** Custom domains are deferred without acknowledging the one-way door (FR-21, FR-7, §7.1 row 11) — passkeys are "bound to that Tenant's exact subdomain", and each Tenant's issuer comes from that subdomain, so v2 custom domains could orphan every passkey and change issuers. FR-7 and FR-10 also allow renaming without saying whether the subdomain changes. *Fix:* state that subdomains are immutable, or choose the production hostname and relying-party ID strategy now, and record the v2 migration consequence.
- **[medium]** Unstated assumptions sit behind some mitigations (§9, FR-6) — "provider status in health view" isn't in FR-6, "review of every authentication story" has no requirement and no reviewer beyond the author, and "ownership of data access defined in architecture" isn't assigned. *Fix:* make each mitigation a requirement, or mark it as not yet committed.
- **[low]** Silent Dashboard gaps (§7.1 row 2, FR-43, UJ-1) — the "quickstart tab" appears only in the scope table, with no FR behind it. Where Admin invitations are accepted is also unclear, since §7.2 defers "invitation". *Fix:* add a consequence for the tab and state the Admin invite flow.

## Downstream usability — thin
This matters most here, because §0 names UX, architecture and stories as consumers. The mechanics are good: IDs are contiguous, the Glossary is real, UJs have protagonists, sections mostly stand alone, and references to the design and research files resolve. The domain model is where it fails. Architecture would have to invent who owns a Deployment, how Admins sign in and where their events go, which are foundations rather than details.

### Findings
- **[critical]** Admin identity and Deployment-level authority are undefined (Glossary Admin and Operator, FR-2, FR-7, FR-39, §11 Q3) — roles are "per Tenant", yet FR-7 has "An Admin with the Owner role" creating Tenants, so authority to create a Tenant or delete the last one has no scope to live in. FR-2 creates the first Admin apparently before any Tenant exists. No FR covers how Admins authenticate, and "Factor" is defined as enrolled by a User, while Admins aren't Users. FR-2 and FR-39 still require them to enrol one. *Fix:* add a Deployment-level role or define a root Tenant, specify Admin sign-in as a requirement, state whether Admins are Users of a system Tenant, and answer Q3.
- **[high]** "Admin" names both a person and a role, with no permission matrix (Glossary Admin, FR-39, FR-10 onward) — a Viewer is an Admin under the Glossary, and so every "An Admin can…" FR grants a Viewer write access. Owner and Viewer aren't in the Glossary, and FR-42's machine-to-machine access has no reconciled place in FR-8's "without an Admin role in that Tenant". *Fix:* rename either the person or the role, add a capability-by-role table, and amend FR-8 to include authorized machine-to-machine Applications.
- **[medium]** Logs have no home for Deployment events (Glossary Log, FR-2, FR-5, FR-7) — a Log is "an event in a Tenant", but recovery, bootstrap and Tenant creation or deletion happen above any Tenant, and FR-5 requires recovery to be logged. *Fix:* define a Deployment-level Log and how it is accessed.
- **[medium]** Admin Dashboard address is unspecified (FR-7, FR-8) — each Tenant has a subdomain, but the Dashboard and Management API hosts aren't stated, which affects cookie scope and cross-Tenant requests. *Fix:* state where each surface is served.
- **[low]** "Realizes" links are loose (§4.6, §4.9, §4.11) — §4.6 claims UJ-2, but UJ-2 contains no Admin action, and neither FR-32 nor FR-41 appears in any UJ. *Fix:* correct the links or add a short Admin journey.

## Shape fit — strong
A greenfield, chain-top product with a real end user is well served by five short journeys plus an FR-level capability spec. Two UJ styles work together: Esm's are operational, including rare but crucial failure cases (UJ-3, UJ-4), and Maya's is consumer. The rigor suits a personal project that handles strangers' credentials, and the PRD says why in §1. The one over-formalization, multi-Admin for one operator, is covered under Strategic coherence.

- **Assumptions roundtrip:** all 11 inline tags are indexed and all index entries appear inline. Two exceptions need attention. A-6 differs in meaning, since inline says "needs revisiting" while §12 says "is achievable on the on-premises server". §11 Q2's "Assumed:" has no tag. - **IDs:** FR-1 to FR-44, NFR-1 to NFR-19, UJ-1 to UJ-5, SM-1 to SM-7 and SM-C1 to SM-C3 are contiguous and unique. Cross-references resolve, and the `research-landscape.md` and `design/` files exist.
- **Glossary drift:** lower-case "sessions" appears in UJ-3 and FR-8. UJ-5 uses "scopes" for Permissions. FR-12 uses "client secret" and "credential" interchangeably. "Developers" (§4.10), "Owner", "Viewer", "Prod Tenant", "phishing-resistant Factor", "passkey", "token family" and "RBAC" are used without Glossary entries.
- **Scope wording:** FR-34's "custom domains" exclusion has no release tag, unlike the other Out of Scope notes, although §7.1 gives one. §7.2 says "breached password warning" while FR-29 forces a change. §6's "An SMS or voice provider user" reads awkwardly.
- **Required sections:** all present for this stakes level and product type. No Acceptance section is needed, since Consequences carry that role, except at FR-25. Frontmatter status is `draft`.
